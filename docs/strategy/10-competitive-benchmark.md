# Tilth — Competitive Benchmark (Deliverable A)

Sources: `docs/research/09-evidence-research-register.md` (Evidence and Research Register),
`docs/product-excellence-audit-2026-11.md`, `docs/engineering-audit-2026-11-18.md`,
and direct inspection of `src/`. Products are reference points, not feature checklists.
"Belongs in Tilth?" answers against the product principles (execution over planning,
simplicity, speed, recovery, offline reliability, coherent identity) — not against a
feature count.

## Synthesis

The highest-value gaps are **not missing features**. Tilth already implements the
transferable principles of the products above: quick add (Todoist/Things 3),
capture-from-anywhere (Akiflow/Linear), guided daily planning (Sunsama),
habits+focus+calendar integration (TickTick), local-first ownership (Obsidian),
lightweight gamification (Habitica). What competitors get wrong — setup trap
(Notion), invasive auto-scheduling (Motion), subscription fatigue and
cancellation complaints (Akiflow/Motion), background-timer bugs (TickTick),
mobile lag and offline limits (Notion), gamification decay (Habitica) — are
precisely the failure modes Tilth's architecture is designed to avoid.

**Conclusion:** Tilth's competitive advantage is not feature parity. It is being
fast, local-first, subscription-free, Arabic-first, and forgiving — while
competitors charge subscriptions, require accounts, need backends, and punish
missed days. The roadmap should deepen capture speed, recovery, and execution
rather than add modules.
