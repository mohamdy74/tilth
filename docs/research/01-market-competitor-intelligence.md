# Deliverable 1 — Market and Competitor Intelligence

Structured comparison of the productivity products studied, their strengths,
weaknesses, target users, differentiators, and important UX decisions. Evidence
is traceable to Deliverable 9 (Evidence and Research Register).

## How to read this

Each product entry covers: primary problem, audience/positioning, strongest
capabilities, praised UX, criticized workflows, onboarding, mobile vs desktop,
performance/reliability, integrations, monetization, retention, differentiators,
weaknesses/gaps. A cross-product capability matrix follows.

Products studied: Todoist, TickTick, Things 3, Microsoft To Do, Google Tasks,
Amazing Marvin, Notion, Obsidian, ClickUp, Asana, Trello, Sunsama, Akiflow,
Motion, Reclaim, Structured, Habitica, Loop Habit Tracker, Forest, RescueTime.

## Task management

### Todoist

- **Primary problem:** Capture and organize tasks across platforms with minimal friction.
- **Audience:** Individuals and small teams; minimalists who want power when needed.
- **Strongest:** Best-in-class natural-language input ("Submit report every Friday at 2pm #work p1"), complex recurrence parsing, near-instant real-time sync, filters/labels, cross-platform (web, iOS, Android, Mac, Windows).
- **Praised UX:** NLP "top tier" (T7); quick capture a "total game changer" (T7); instant sync with Google Calendar (T8).
- **Criticized:** Free plan "far too stingy" (T5); some find it "complex, counterintuitive" (T9); Pro $60/yr (T5).
- **Onboarding:** Low for basic capture; filters/labels have a learning curve.
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Strong on both; native apps.
- **Performance:** Fast capture; sync is a noted strength.
- **Integrations:** Broad (Google Calendar, etc.).
- **Monetization:** Freemium; Pro $5/mo or $60/yr.
- **Retention:** Habit of daily capture; cross-platform lock-in.
- **Differentiator:** NLP speed and recurrence parsing.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** Subscription cost; complexity for simple users; no built-in focus timer/habits in core.

### TickTick

- **Primary problem:** One app to run the day — tasks + calendar + habits + Pomodoro.
- **Audience:** Students, creators, solo users who want structure in one place.
- **Strongest:** Calendar view, Pomodoro, habit tracker, notes, Eisenhower Matrix, cheap premium.
- **Praised:** "Best for all-in-one personal productivity"; NLP effective; Pomodoro + white noise used daily.
- **Criticized:** Feature expansion "can tempt you to spend more time configuring your system than completing tasks" (Nerdynav); premium required for calendar/filters; Pomodoro stops in background (T10); calendar view tag-filter bug on Mac (T11); cannot create calendar events from calendar view (T12); inbox items reappear after dating (T13).
- **Onboarding:** Moderate — many features to discover.
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Good both; calendar view inconsistencies across platforms.
- **Performance:** Generally reliable; background timer issue.
- **Integrations:** Calendar sync (Google/Outlook); AI via TickTick GPT.
- **Monetization:** Free tier generous; Premium from ~$3.99/mo ($35.99/yr).
- **Retention:** Habit + Pomodoro + calendar stickiness.
- **Differentiator:** All-in-one tasks/habits/focus/calendar at low price.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** No calendar-event creation; background timer; config overload risk.

### Things 3

- **Primary problem:** Calm, elegant personal task management for Apple users.
- **Audience:** Solo Apple-ecosystem users who value design and one-time pricing.
- **Strongest:** Best-in-class design/interaction; instant open (<1s); Quick Entry under a second from any app; Today/Evening/Upcoming/Anytime/Someday; areas/projects/headings; Apple Shortcuts/Siri/widgets; iCloud sync.
- **Praised:** "Opening Things on Mac is instant... feels like cheating" vs subscription apps (T2); "calm, polished interface"; love Today view and evening "when" feature (T4).
- **Criticized:** Apple-only (no Web/Android/Windows/ Linux); no collaboration; no NLP input; no location reminders; no web interface (PCMag); menus "awkward to navigate at times"; rigid repeating tasks.
- **Onboarding:** Low — few decisions.
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Mac is "where the real work happens"; iPhone is capture net; fast and reliable iCloud sync.
- **Performance:** Instant open; no loading state creating tasks — a benchmark for responsiveness.
- **Integrations:** Apple ecosystem only (Shortcuts, Siri, URL scheme, AppleScript); no API.
- **Monetization:** One-time purchase: Mac $49.99, iPhone $9.99, iPad $19.99 (~$80 total). No subscription.
- **Retention:** Users keep it for years (one reviewer: 4 years daily driver).
- **Differentiator:** Design calm + speed + one-time ownership.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** Platform lock-in; no team features; no AI; no web access.

### Microsoft To Do

- **Primary problem:** Simple, free personal task lists inside the Microsoft ecosystem.
- **Audience:** Microsoft 365 / Outlook users; personal use.
- **Strongest:** Outlook tasks integration; "My Day" daily personalized suggestions; free; cross-platform (web, iOS, Android, Windows, Mac); clean friendly interface; shared lists.
- **Praised:** Quick to add tasks; free.
- **Criticized:** "Those few extra seconds add up" per task creation (ZDNET); plain/simple; limited for power users; hasn't caught up with Microsoft's AI pivot (Zapier); no real team collaboration (no assignment).
- **Onboarding:** Very low.
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Good both; native Windows app.
- **Performance:** Reliable; simple.
- **Integrations:** Outlook, Microsoft 365.
- **Monetization:** Free.
- **Retention:** Ecosystem lock-in for Microsoft users.
- **Differentiator:** Free + Outlook integration + My Day.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** Barebones; no advanced features; team collaboration weak.

### Google Tasks

- **Primary problem:** Minimal task tracking inside Gmail/Google Calendar.
- **Audience:** Google Workspace / Gmail users wanting digital minimalism.
- **Strongest:** Lives in Gmail and Calendar sidebars; free; drag Gmail emails to tasks; simple.
- **Praised:** Minimal; fast for Google ecosystem users.
- **Criticized:** "Fairly barebones" (Zapier); no time-based reminders, no file attachments, no tags, no sharing (TasksBoard); no native sharing at all.
- **Onboarding:** Near zero.
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Dedicated mobile app; sidebar on desktop.
- **Performance:** Fast; simple.
- **Integrations:** Gmail, Google Calendar.
- **Monetization:** Free.
- **Retention:** Ecosystem habit.
- **Differentiator:** Zero-friction capture from Gmail/Calendar.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** Too minimal for power users; no sharing; no reminders of its own.

### Amazing Marvin

- **Primary problem:** Task management that adapts to how your brain works (ADHD-focused).
- **Audience:** Procrastinators, ADHD users, people who want to enable/disable features.
- **Strongest:** 100+ features; toggleable "strategies" (prioritization, time tracking, rewards, eat-the-frog); multi-level organization; import from other apps; master list → drag to day.
- **Praised:** "Hitting the sweet spot"; easy data entry via top bar; anti-procrastination features.
- **Criticized:** Customization "clumsy and confusing... a lot to take in" (AppSumo); "Omnifocus friction trap: a lot of clicking"; not native (web tech); mobile "falls short" (MPU Talk); $12/mo.
- **Onboarding:** High — 100+ features is overwhelming.
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Web-tech desktop; mobile usable but not mobile-first.
- **Performance:** Web-tech; not native-feeling.
- **Integrations:** Import from Things/OmniFocus; integrations page.
- **Monetization:** Free trial; ~$12/mo (yearly cheaper); student discount.
- **Retention:** Feature fit for ADHD workflows.
- **Differentiator:** Enable/disable strategies; built for starting/following through.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** Overwhelming complexity; clunky UI; mobile weakness; subscription cost.

## All-in-one & daily planning

### Notion

- **Primary problem:** One workspace for notes, databases, tasks, wiki — build your own system.
- **Audience:** System-builders, teams, knowledge workers; personal "second brain."
- **Strongest:** Flexible databases; blocks; templates; AI (writing, search, agents); docs + databases in one structure.
- **Praised:** Powerful; "best in class" databases; does many things reasonably well.
- **Criticized:** "Setup trap" — users spend days tweaking instead of working (P2, P4); too heavy/slow for quick capture (P1); mobile performance degrades with large databases; offline requires manual page downloads with 50-row cap (G2); "aesthetic trap" (P6); laggy startup (C2).
- **Onboarding:** High — blank canvas, steep learning curve.
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Desktop capable; mobile laggy with large data.
- **Performance:** Slows with large pages/databases; startup friction.
- **Integrations:** Slack, GitHub, HubSpot (via Notion AI agents); broad.
- **Monetization:** Freemium; AI is a paid add-on; per-seat.
- **Retention:** Data gravity once a workspace is built.
- **Differentiator:** Infinite flexibility; all-in-one.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** Speed of capture; complexity; mobile performance; offline limits; subscription.

### Obsidian

- **Primary problem:** Private, local-first Markdown knowledge base.
- **Audience:** Knowledge workers, researchers, privacy-conscious users.
- **Strongest:** Local-first (files on device); Markdown; graph view; plugin ecosystem; no account required; fast search; instant.
- **Praised:** "No pop-ups, no major overhauls"; data remains yours; fast (P5).
- **Criticized:** Steep learning curve (Markdown + plugins); manual maintenance; connections require deliberate effort; sync requires paid sub or workarounds; no real-time collaboration; no iPhone for Loop (different app, but pattern).
- **Onboarding:** Moderate-high.
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Both; mobile app exists.
- **Performance:** Fast; local files.
- **Integrations:** Community plugins; sync is paid (Obsidian Sync).
- **Monetization:** Free personal; $50/yr commercial; Sync paid.
- **Retention:** Data ownership; plugin ecosystem.
- **Differentiator:** Local-first privacy; Markdown files.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** Setup effort; no built-in tasks/calendar depth; sync cost; no collaboration.

### ClickUp

- **Primary problem:** Replace all work tools with one platform.
- **Audience:** Teams, complex workflows, project management.
- **Strongest:** Tasks, docs, whiteboards, goals, time tracking; multiple views (list, board, Gantt); ultra-customizable.
- **Praised:** Powerful; grows with needs.
- **Criticized:** "Too complicated for its main use"; "missing the last 10-15% in every category"; "wasn't intuitive AT ALL"; switched back to Asana (P9, P10).
- **Onboarding:** High — overwhelming.
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Both; complexity on both.
- **Performance:** Heavy.
- **Integrations:** Broad (it's a platform).
- **Monetization:** Freemium; Starter $7; AI add-on $9.
- **Retention:** Team data gravity.
- **Differentiator:** All-in-one for teams.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** Complexity; feature overload; not personal-productivity focused.

### Asana

- **Primary problem:** Team project and goal management.
- **Audience:** Teams, managers.
- **Strongest:** Goals, milestones, reporting; easy to pick up; clean.
- **Praised:** "SUPER easy to pick up" vs ClickUp (P10).
- **Criticized:** Team-focused, overkill for personal use.
- **Onboarding:** Low-moderate.
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Both.
- **Performance:** Reliable.
- **Integrations:** Broad.
- **Monetization:** Freemium; per-seat.
- **Retention:** Team workflows.
- **Differentiator:** Goals/reporting for teams.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** Not for solo personal productivity; cost scales with team.

### Trello

- **Primary problem:** Visual kanban task tracking.
- **Audience:** Small teams, simple workflows.
- **Strongest:** Fastest to adopt; cork-board intuition; no setup burden.
- **Praised:** Intuitive; fast onboarding.
- **Criticized:** Simplicity "runs out as projects grow"; "good for small teams, not growing teams" (G2).
- **Onboarding:** Near zero.
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Both; simple.
- **Performance:** Fast.
- **Integrations:** Power-ups.
- **Monetization:** Freemium.
- **Retention:** Simplicity habit.
- **Differentiator:** Kanban simplicity.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** Doesn't scale to complex projects; personal productivity depth limited.

## Daily planning / time blocking

### Sunsama

- **Primary problem:** Guided, deliberate daily planning ritual.
- **Audience:** Busy professionals who want a calmer, intentional day.
- **Strongest:** Daily planning flow; weekly review; end-of-day review; tie tasks to "objectives"; integrations (Notion, Asana, Trello, ClickUp, GitHub, Gmail).
- **Praised:** "Fewest downsides" for multi-planner refugees (UseCarly); mindful, calm; morning ritual.
- **Criticized:** "The ritual can feel slow" (Akiflow blog); mobile app "isn't great"; not for phone planning or offline (UseCarly); no task prioritization (uses objectives); manual-only, no AI scheduling (Wirecutter); $22/mo.
- **Onboarding:** Moderate — vocabulary needs acclimation.
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Desktop strong; mobile weak.
- **Performance:** Reliable.
- **Integrations:** Many task/calendar sources.
- **Monetization:** ~$22/mo (annual ~$17/mo); no free tier (discontinued per Temporal).
- **Retention:** Morning-ritual habit; "people who actually do the morning ritual rarely leave."
- **Differentiator:** Guided deliberate planning vs automation.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** Price; mobile; offline; no auto-scheduling; no prioritization.

### Akiflow

- **Primary problem:** Fast capture + connected task sources + calendar execution.
- **Audience:** Professionals consolidating work from multiple tools into a calendar.
- **Strongest:** Keyboard-first capture; universal inbox (task consolidation); drag task to calendar timebox; Superhuman-like command bar.
- **Praised:** Fast capture; connected sources; keyboard control.
- **Criticized:** $34/mo ($19/yr) — expensive; "unexpected charges and difficult cancellations"; less centered on integrations than Sunsama; mobile better than Sunsama but still.
- **Onboarding:** Moderate.
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Desktop keyboard-first; mobile usable.
- **Performance:** Fast capture.
- **Integrations:** Many (Gmail, etc.).
- **Monetization:** ~$34/mo or $19/yr.
- **Retention:** Capture habit.
- **Differentiator:** Universal task inbox + calendar execution.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** Price; cancellation complaints; no auto-scheduling.

### Motion

- **Primary problem:** AI auto-schedules tasks and meetings onto your calendar.
- **Audience:** People who want software to build/rebuild their schedule.
- **Strongest:** AI auto-scheduling; task prioritization; calendar + tasks + projects in one; team features.
- **Praised:** Auto-populating schedule; useful if you struggle with time management.
- **Criticized:** "Aggressive auto-rescheduling... rebuilds it every time reality gets in the way... invasive" (Medium); over-schedules, "packs their day too tightly... oppressive" (alfred_); $19-29/mo, no free plan, card-required trial; not for manual curators.
- **Onboarding:** Moderate-high.
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Both; calendar-centric.
- **Performance:** Reliable.
- **Integrations:** Calendar; less than Sunsama (no task consolidation from other apps).
- **Monetization:** $19-29/mo; no free tier.
- **Retention:** Automation habit; price-driven exits to Reclaim.
- **Differentiator:** AI auto-scheduling.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** Price; rigidity/invasiveness; over-scheduling; no free tier.

### Reclaim

- **Primary problem:** Defend focus time and habits on your calendar; smart meeting scheduling.
- **Audience:** Calendar-centric users; teams with 1:1s.
- **Strongest:** Habit/focus time defense; auto-schedules around existing calendar; 1:1 scheduling links; free Lite tier; Google + Outlook.
- **Praised:** Best value for defending time; free plan real; cheaper than Motion.
- **Criticized:** Can "flood a calendar with events hard to remove"; meeting coordination with outsiders weak; manages calendar not conversations.
- **Onboarding:** Moderate.
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Calendar-centric; both.
- **Performance:** Responds to conflicts in ~15 seconds.
- **Integrations:** Google Calendar, Outlook.
- **Monetization:** Free Lite; paid $8-18/seat/mo.
- **Retention:** Focus-time habit.
- **Differentiator:** Calendar-as-source-of-truth (negotiates around, doesn't own the day).
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** External scheduling weak; calendar flood risk.

### Structured

- **Primary problem:** Clean visual timeline of the day.
- **Audience:** Visual day-planning users.
- **Strongest:** Visual timeline; time-blocking; simple.
- **Praised:** Clean visual day view.
- **Criticized:** Niche; less ecosystem.
- **Monetization:** Freemium.
- **Differentiator:** Visual timeline day view.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** Smaller feature set; less known.

## Habits & focus

### Habitica

- **Primary problem:** Gamify tasks and habits via RPG.
- **Audience:** Gamification-motivated users; social accountability.
- **Strongest:** RPG character, gems, armor, quests; habits/dailies/todos; groups/parties; free core.
- **Praised:** Engaging format; "ridiculously effective" for some (App Store); group motivation.
- **Criticized:** "Messy and difficult to navigate"; bugs (rewards page disappears, crashes daily, habits laggy) (App Store); gamification motivation fades after newness (Everyday.app); can feel noisy for self-care.
- **Onboarding:** Moderate (RPG concepts).
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Both; mobile primary.
- **Performance:** Crashes reported.
- **Integrations:** Limited.
- **Monetization:** Free core; optional subscriptions.
- **Retention:** Gamification (short-term push).
- **Differentiator:** Gamified RPG loop.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** Bugs; navigation clutter; motivation decay; not calm.

### Loop Habit Tracker

- **Primary problem:** Simple, private habit tracking.
- **Audience:** Android users wanting a no-frills habit tracker.
- **Strongest:** Clean, simple, "only what is needed"; ad-free; open source; no limits; detailed charts; habit score; privacy-respecting; home-screen widgets.
- **Praised:** 4.9★, 63.7K reviews (Google Play); "clean, simple."
- **Criticized:** Android-only (no iPhone); requests: all-habits graph, categories.
- **Onboarding:** Near zero.
- **Mobile vs desktop:** Mobile (Android) only.
- **Performance:** Fast; local.
- **Integrations:** None (local).
- **Monetization:** Free (open source).
- **Retention:** Simplicity; no cost.
- **Differentiator:** Completely free, private, simple.
- **Weaknesses/gaps:** Android-only; no iOS; no focus/tasks integration.

### Forest / RescueTime

- **Forest:** Focus timer via gamified tree-growing; phone lock-in. Good for distraction aversion; narrow scope (focus only).
- **RescueTime:** Automatic time tracking; distraction blocking; category reports; goal/alert management. Automatic (no manual logging); desktop + mobile; SaaS.

## What Tilth already does well vs competitors (verified in code)

1. **Instant local-first operation** — IndexedDB, boot after hydration, skeletons until ready. Comparable to Things 3's instant open; better than Notion's laggy startup.
2. **Quick-add with inline syntax** — `!high`, `#tag`, date words incl. Arabic. Comparable to Todoist NLP but lighter and dependency-free.
3. **Kanban + list + saved views** — Trello-like board plus Todoist-like filters; rare in personal apps.
4. **Habits + streaks with skip-limit recovery** — More forgiving than rigid streak apps; comparable to Loop but integrated with tasks.
5. **Focus timer with timestamp correctness** — Survives reloads/background tabs (unlike TickTick's background Pomodoro bug).
6. **No subscription, local-first data ownership** — Direct answer to subscription fatigue (C5-C7); data never leaves device unless user opts into encrypted sync.
7. **Arabic-first RTL** — Unique differentiator; no major competitor defaults to Arabic.
